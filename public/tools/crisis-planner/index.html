The plan in brief

This plan is for your company, a SaaS company with B2B enterprise customers, medium (business data) data sensitivity and a scale-up (50 to 200) team. You describe the company as "our company". You named no crises, so it covers a default set for SaaS, not ranked by likelihood: service outage, data breach, security vulnerability and customer data exposure. Every time in this plan (minutes, hours, days) is an example to replace with your own.

Who runs the response

The incident commander is the CEO or a designated VP. The core team is always activated: CTO, VP Customer Success, VP Marketing or Communications and Legal. The extended team is brought in as needed: HR, Engineering lead and Support lead.

The plan names roles, not people, because no names were given: before a crisis, write down a person, a deputy, a phone number and an email for each role, and keep the list where the team can reach it when systems are down. Measure every incident by time to detect, time to restore, customers affected and messages sent. In every crisis, tell employees the facts and what not to say outside, before the media can.

Playbooks
Service outage

What it looks like here: an outage stops customers' daily work in the product, and their own customers or teams see the effect.

Severity levels (the response time is the time to respond, an example to replace)

LevelWhat it meansRespond withinEscalate to
SEV-1A complete outage for all customers15 minutesCEO or a designated VP
SEV-2A major function down for many customers30 minutesCTO
SEV-3Degraded performance1 hourEngineering lead

First fifteen minutes

  1. Acknowledge on the status page, or to your customers' service owners: "Investigating reports of a problem with the service of your company".
  2. Assemble the incident room: engineering, support and account owners.
  3. Start the diagnosis and name the owner of the fix.
  4. Brief the support and account teams, so they are ready for the volume.

While it lasts

AfterUpdateChannel
15 minutes"Identified", with a one-line plain description of the causeStatus page, and a direct update from the account owner
30 minutes"Working on a fix", with the time of the next updateStatus page, and a direct update from the account owner
60 minutesProgress, or a revised timeStatus page and an email to the affected
Every 30 minutesA further update until it is resolvedStatus page

Who to tell

  • Technology or service owners at customers (Account team): a call from the account owner, then the status page; say which functions are affected, the workaround if there is one, and the time of the next update.
  • The customers' own support teams (Account team): a note through the channel agreed in advance; say what their users will see and what to tell them.
Security incident: data breach (also covers: customer data exposure)

What could be exposed here: exposure of customer account data, credentials or the data customers store in the product.

How to rate it

FactorHighMediumLow
Data exposedPersonal or financial data, or credentialsBusiness dataNo customer data
Customers affectedMany, or a large accountSomeA few
AttackStill goingUnknownContained

First four hours

  1. Activate the response team: CEO or a designated VP as incident commander.
  2. Contain the threat: isolate the affected systems and revoke compromised credentials.
  3. Preserve evidence: take forensic images before any repair.
  4. Start the incident log: timeline, actions and decisions.
  5. Keep communication internal until the facts are checked.
  6. Close the access if customer data was open to others (the open storage, link, permission or report), and preserve the access logs before any clean-up, so you can say who looked.

Hours four to twenty-four

  1. Establish the scope: what data, how many customers, for how long.
  2. Find the attack vector and close it.
  3. Bring in forensics, internal or external.
  4. Check which of your contracts or compliance duties require notification; the duties and deadlines differ by law and country, and counsel confirms them.
  5. Draft the customer message and hold it until counsel has approved it.

Who to tell

  • Regulators (Legal): a formal filing; say the notification counsel has approved.
  • Enterprise accounts (Account team): a call from the account owner, then a written note; say what happened, what you are doing, what they should do.
  • Media, if needed (Communications lead): a short statement; say the facts only.
Security vulnerability

What it means here: a flaw in your product or tooling that someone outside has found, or that you have found, and that has not yet been used against a customer, as far as you know.

First four hours

  1. Confirm it and rate it by real exposure (who can reach it and what it gives access to), not by label alone.
  2. Search the logs for signs that it was already used; if it was, move to the data breach steps.
  3. Decide the stop-gap (a feature switch, a rule, an access change or a patch) and who approves it.
  4. Name the owner: CEO or a designated VP as sponsor, with CTO as technical owner.

Fix and advisory

  1. Fix, test and release; keep the rating in the advisory the same as in your internal record.
  2. Write the advisory: what is affected, what is fixed, how a customer checks, and what they do.
  3. Check which of your contracts or compliance duties require notice of a vulnerability or a fix; the duties and deadlines differ by law and country, and counsel confirms them.

Who to tell

  • Reporter, if a researcher found it (Security lead): direct reply; say receipt, one owner, the timing of the fix.
  • Affected customers (Account team): a call from the account owner, then the advisory; say what is affected, the fix, how to check.
Rules for every message

Be fast with the first facts and honest about what you do not yet know. Acknowledge the impact before you explain it. Be specific, because vague statements erode trust. Use one spokesperson and one set of facts.

To sharpen this, give:

  • the crises you have seen or fear, in the potential crises input (it would change the playbooks, which are now a default set for the sector)
  • the compliance items that apply to you, in the compliance requirements input (it would change the notification steps, which now say "each requirement you have")
  • the business model, in the how you charge input (it would change the severity levels and the words used for credits and service levels)

Who is the Crisis Planner tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want finished plans ready to publish: each tool returns a structured draft, with placeholders where your inputs gave no fact.
  • Anyone who needs live market or competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type, and anything they add themselves is marked as an example, a suggestion or a placeholder.

How does the Crisis Planner tool work?

You do not need to sign in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Crisis Planner.
