Objection handlers

Objections vertical SaaS buyers often raise that you did not list
  • We run on spreadsheets and it works: Map one real week of work in their spreadsheets, show where time and errors go, and let them judge the product against their own process.
  • Our people will not change how they work: Offer a rollout with one team first, training in their own words, and an adoption measure agreed before the start.
  • It will not fit how our industry works: Walk through the few workflows that are particular to the trade, using their own documents and forms, and say what is configured and what is built.
Who decides in vertical SaaS

The owner, managing director or head of operations signs; the department head who feels the daily problem champions it; the people who work in it every day decide whether it sticks; finance checks cost and payback; IT checks the fit with the systems already in place. A proof point that lands: one team or site before and after on the work the software changes, measured by the buyer's own staff over a full working cycle.

How to read the answer

The tool does not look up live competitor data; it organizes what you typed into Where you stand, a Comparison evidence to confirm table, per-competitor battle cards (lead with, discovery questions, and Why not stay with the current approach?), and Objection handlers. Every strength or weakness you entered is marked Seller claim, unverified and Not established until you and the buyer test both options on the same buyer task with a dated source. Use the battle cards as questions to ask the buyer and tests to run in a live evaluation, not as claims to read out as settled facts.

What to do next

  • Founder: Review the Comparison evidence to confirm table and pick one buyer criterion where you can prove your advantage on the buyer's own data or workflow.
  • CEO: Check Where you stand and Why not stay with the current approach? to ensure your positioning addresses the status quo and manual work, not just named software rivals.
  • CMO: Add the missing inputs listed under To sharpen this (competitor details, recent wins, and recent losses with reasons) so the battle cards reflect real deal evidence.
  • CRO: Coach reps to ask the neutral discovery questions under each competitor card instead of disparaging rivals, and require a live proof on the buyer's task.
  • Demand generation head: Use the Objections <sector> buyers often raise that you did not list to build comparison guides and mid-funnel content that answer those concerns early.

Why come back

Come back after every quarterly win/loss review or whenever a new competitor appears in your pipeline, and re-run the tool with your latest won and lost deal notes to update your battle cards.

Who is the Competitive Intel tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want finished plans ready to publish: each tool returns a structured draft, with placeholders where your inputs gave no fact.
  • Anyone who needs live market or competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type, and anything they add themselves is marked as an example, a suggestion or a placeholder.

How does the Competitive Intel tool work?

You do not need to sign in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Competitive Intel.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the CRAFT GTM code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
