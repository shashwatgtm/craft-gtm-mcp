Launch Commander

Build a launch plan with a timeline

For product marketers and CMOs. The decision: what is our launch plan, week by week?

Give it what you are launching, the launch type, target segments and goals. You get a launch timeline, messaging for each segment, success metrics, risks and a prelaunch checklist.

Last updated 5 November 2026

How do I plan a B2B product launch backwards from revenue and pipeline targets?

Enter what you are launching, the launch type (major release, feature launch, beta launch, product update, or market expansion), target segments, and goals in Launch Commander, along with an optional launch date, channels, team size, and budget level. The tool files each goal you enter under its own metric, outlines segment messaging and channel priorities, and builds a launch timeline in phases set by the launch type (Prep, Build, Launch, and Follow-up for a feature launch), dated from your launch date or in planning mode when TBD, plus risks and a pre-launch checklist.

What the goals need. The target is 40 brand demos, about 20 in each of 2 segments if you share them evenly (40 divided by 2, rounded up).

Who the launch speaks to

The launch lead should confirm the buyer and approver before choosing whom to brief. Proof to collect from one customer in each segment: secondary sales or productive calls in the pilot region against a comparable region without the product. Next step to ask for: Agree a pilot in one region with a set of distributors, with a measured comparison region.

Problems to test in the first conversations: reps who capture orders on paper or in several apps, so secondary sales are seen late; trade schemes that are not communicated to reps or checked in the outlet; distributor stock and order data that are out of sync with the company's view. Questions to ask in any segment:

  • How do your reps capture orders in the outlet today, and how does that reach the distributor?
  • How do you see secondary sales by outlet and by SKU today, and how late is that view?
  • What share of your outlets does a rep cover in a typical beat, and who plans the beats?
Packaged food brands

For Packaged food brands, lead with "Order suggestions for field reps". The launch lead should confirm who approves a purchase in Packaged food brands before briefing the segment, then record the named contact and the evidence they require. Measure the result by order fill rate.

Personal care brands

Nothing in your description points to Personal care brands more than to another segment, so open with "Order suggestions for field reps", the first part you list, and move to the part their answers point to. The launch lead should confirm who approves a purchase in Personal care brands before briefing the segment, then record the named contact and the evidence they require. Measure the result by strike rate.

Words this buyer uses, to use in the copy: distributor, outlet, beat plan, secondary sales, general trade and SKU.

Channels and budget

The channels are the usual ones for vertical SaaS, FMCG retail execution buyers: account-based email to sales heads and sales operations, LinkedIn posts from your experts and customers, field visit to a pilot region and industry and sales leaders meetups. At a moderate budget, prioritise account-based outreach to named accounts, a small executive roundtable, posts from customers and your own experts and targeted ads to named accounts.

How to read the answer

The output turns your feature description, launch type, target segments, and goals into The plan in brief, Goals and how each is measured (splitting numerical targets evenly across segments as an illustration), Who the launch speaks to, Channels and budget, a Confirm remaining delivery capacity check, a four-phase Timeline (Prep, Build, Launch, and Follow-up), Risks and how to meet them, and a Pre-launch checklist. Tasks whose suggested dates fall before today are marked Overdue if unfinished; completion unknown, and the reference schedule does not establish that your team has the capacity to finish them by launch day.

What to do next

  • Founder: Check Confirm remaining delivery capacity and move the launch date or cut scope if Prep and Build tasks are still unfinished inside two weeks of launch.
  • CEO: Verify that Risks and how to meet them has a named engineering owner for the rollback plan and one named owner for post-launch adoption tracking.
  • CMO: Collect one before-and-after customer proof point for each target segment as required under Who the launch speaks to, and lock positioning before asset production starts.
  • CRO: Train the sales team on the segment messages and battle cards before launch day, and track qualified opportunities in the CRM separately from closed revenue.
  • Demand generation head: Assign owners and dates to the Build and Launch tasks (Landing page update, Email sequences, Blog post, Announcement email, and Social posts) and verify tracking for each goal in the table.

Why come back

Re-run the tool with your sector, business model, and available channels filled in (as listed under To sharpen this), or come back for your next feature release, beta launch, or market expansion. Who is the Launch Commander tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that want finished plans ready to publish: each tool returns a structured draft, with placeholders where your inputs gave no fact.
  • Anyone who needs live market or competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type, and anything they add themselves is marked as an example, a suggestion or a placeholder.
