Scale Paid Check

Should we scale paid now?

For founders, CMOs, CROs and their teams. The decision: which customer segments can earn more paid budget, and which need work first.

Scale Paid Check uses your customer list to review retained customers, revenue kept and paid acquisition payback for one segment. Supply the cohort dates, fees and your own decision lines. Read the result with its evidence limits, then use the retention and interview steps to check what should change before increasing spend.

Choose your app

  • Claude: Add the CRAFT GTM connector (https://craft-gtm.gtmhelix.com/mcp) in Claude under Customize and Connectors. Ask "Should we scale paid now?" and Claude runs the same check. The guided chat, one question at a time with the upload echo before the call, is in the Gemini skill only.

  • Gemini: In Gemini CLI and Antigravity, install the Helix GTM Suite extension (https://github.com/shashwatgtm/helix-gtm-suite) and run the should-we-scale-paid skill, which walks through the five questions, cleans mixed date formats, merged cells, and currency symbols, echoes back rows received, used, and rejected for your confirmation, and calls the CRAFT GTM connector.

What you give the check

In whichever app you use, you give five things:

  1. Your customer list from your billing system or CRM (up to 2,000 rows; if your list is over 2,000 rows, split it by segment and run each part): customer ID, signing date, segment, monthly fee, any stop date, any return date, and, if you have it, each customer's activity in each of the 12 months before the check date, which the tool uses to tag customers seasonal or steady. Use customer IDs only, never customer or company names.
  2. Billing term: monthly or annual.
  3. Your keep line and payback months, chosen before you look at any result: the share of customers a cohort must keep at month 12 and the maximum months for paid acquisition to pay back from gross margin. If you give no lines, the answer uses Shashwat Ghosh's two example lines, not benchmarks: 80% of customers kept at month 12 and a 12 month payback. It says so at the top and asks you to give your own lines and run it again. The Gemini skill asks for your lines before it runs the check.
  4. Churn reasons in customers' own words: the reasons customers gave when they paused or left.
  5. Optional money inputs: gross margin, paid only customer acquisition cost, a 90 day test budget cap, and floor and target win rates. Without these money inputs, the answer can say Hold or Small capped test, never Scale.

How to read the answer

Read the sections from top to bottom before approving next quarter's demand generation plan:

  1. Answer card (verdict first): The first line is the base verdict for your whole customer list. Then each segment and tag gets a single verdict: Scale, Small capped test, Hold or Not enough data yet. The tool never says "you have PMF". When a group passes the keep line with fewer than 15 customers in the group, or when three or fewer extra losses would flip the group to a fail, the answer labels that pass "a lead, not proof" and caps the verdict at Small capped test rather than Scale. While the base verdict is Hold, no group goes beyond Small capped test.
  2. Check this first: Before reading any cohort percentage, check the row counts: rows received, rows used, rows rejected (with the reason each rejected row was dropped), and the count of paying customers and monthly recurring revenue on the check date. Confirm that those numbers match your billing system or CRM. This section also lists pending stops (customers stopped less than 90 days before the check date), paused and returned customers, and which decision lines were used.
  3. Cohort table and stop rule: The cohort table shows each signing quarter at months 3, 6, 9 and 12. The stop rule, printed after the Money section, checks your two latest cohorts that have a full 12 months of history. If either cohort keeps less than your keep line at month 12, or loses as many or more customers in months 7 to 12 as it lost in months 1 to 6, the whole base holds. For annual billing only the month 12 test applies. Younger cohorts are printed in the table so you can read them early, and each row shows how many percentage points a single customer moves that cohort.
  4. Groups table: Splits your 12 month window base by segment and by tag (such as seasonal versus steady) and compares each group's share of customer losses with its share of the customer base. A group that accounts for a larger share of losses than its share of customers is where churn sits.
  5. Money: Caps customer lifetime value at the number of months you have actually observed in your customer list, works out monthly gross margin from your average fee, and checks paid only payback against your payback months. Blended acquisition cost is shown only for contrast so a cheap organic average never hides an expensive paid channel. When a group is ready for a 90 day capped test, this section turns your test budget cap and win rates into exact sales accepted opportunity counts to continue, repeat once, or stop.
  6. First draft responses and churn interview guide: Chains your cohort findings into a first draft Retention Playbook response for each churn reason (signal to watch, first step, owner, draft message, and illustrative health score weights) and a first draft Customer Interview Kit guide so you can interview every customer lost in the 12 month window without leading the witness.

Illustrative worked example

About this example. The figures below are illustrative exercise inputs supplied for the worked example. They show how the method works and do not describe a customer's actual results or strategy.

In the illustrative exercise from Guide 04, 56 illustrative customer rows (50 agencies in the 12 month window at an illustrative $3,000 monthly fee, 70% gross margin, $35,000 paid acquisition cost, $15,000 blended acquisition cost, and a $60,000 budget cap) are tested against the example lines, not benchmarks: 80% of customers kept at month 12 and a 12 month payback. The illustrative base holds at 39 of 50 kept (78%), and the seasonal tag holds at 13 of 20 kept (65%, which is 64% of illustrative losses from 40% of the base). The steady tag clears the keep line at 26 of 30 kept (87%), but because the base holds and three more illustrative losses would fail the group, steady gets a Small capped test verdict ("a lead, not proof") with illustrative 90 day opportunity lines of 16 or more steady opportunities to run a second capped quarter and 8 or fewer to stop. Illustrative paid payback is also 16.7 months against the 12 month line.

What to do next by role

  • Founder or CEO: Approve, cap, or stop the paid budget per customer group based on the Answer card. Put the cohort table and your keep line in front of everyone who signs off on budget in week 1, book the churn and pause interviews within 28 days, and require a named executive signature on day 90 to keep or lift any hold.
  • CMO: Move paid budget out of held groups and into the groups that pass. Where a group earns a Small capped test, brief the team to judge the 90 day test on total cost per sales accepted opportunity in that passing group rather than top line lead volume.
  • CRO: Use the churn reasons and the first draft interview guide with account teams to interview every customer lost in the 12 month window. Have sales acceptance check every incoming opportunity against the passing group profile and keep failing group deals out of the capped test count.
  • Demand generation head: Build the targeting list for the passing group from observable signals before signup, run the 90 day capped test against the opportunity lines from the Money section, and stop the spend if sales accepted opportunities land at or below the stop count.
  • Customer success head: Act on the first draft response to each churn reason by confirming the cause with affected customers first, separating departures whose cause is still unknown, and adding your real product and billing signals to the health score before turning on automated plays.

Why come back

Rerun Scale Paid Check whenever a held group accumulates more months of data under your retention fix, when younger cohorts cross 12 months of history, when paused or pending customers return to paying, or when a 90 day capped test finishes and gives you audited paid acquisition cost and win rates. The tool runs fixed rules in server code, so the same inputs give the same result every time. It does not call an AI model to do the arithmetic, and this site does not store what you enter.

When retention says hold while a growth target or board deadline says go, bring your cohort table, segment split, capped test budget, and keep line to a 45 minute call with Shashwat Ghosh: https://calendly.com/shashwat-gtmhelix/45min

Frequently asked questions

Should we scale paid now?

Scale paid spend only for a customer group that passes both tests in order: first, your two latest mature cohorts with 12 months of history and the customer group itself must pass your keep line at month 12 without losing as many or more customers in months 7 to 12 as in months 1 to 6; second, paid acquisition for that group must pay back within your payback months using customer lifetime value capped at the months you have actually seen. If a group passes retention with fewer than 15 customers, or has not yet proved paid payback, run a 90 day capped test to measure cost per sales accepted opportunity before scaling.

Is churn too high to spend more on ads?

Blended monthly churn often hides whether your recent cohorts are getting worse or whether losses sit inside one customer segment. Scale Paid Check cuts your customer list from your billing system or CRM by signing quarter and by segment, applies a 90 day pause rule, and tests your two latest mature cohorts against your keep line. If the base fails the keep line, raising ad spend across the whole funnel will buy more of the customers who leave, unless one steady group passes on its own and gets a targeted capped test.

Why are customers leaving, and how do we know a churn reason sits in one segment?

Counting how many lost customers mentioned a reason is not enough on its own, because a reason that shows up in 60% of lost customers means nothing if that group is also 60% of your customer base. Scale Paid Check compares each group's share of 12 month losses against its share of your 12 month customer base, then drafts a first response to each churn reason in your customers' own words and a churn interview guide that tests each hypothesis without leading the witness.

What if we do not have paid acquisition cost or gross margin numbers yet?

You can still run the check with only your customer list, billing term, keep line, payback months, and churn reasons. Without gross margin, paid acquisition cost, test budget cap, and win rates, the tool runs the full upload echo, cohort table, stop rule, segment split, retention playbook drafts, and churn interview guide, and its verdict for a passing group will be Small capped test or Hold, never Scale, until paid payback is measured.

Are the 80% keep line at month 12 and the 12 month payback industry benchmarks?

No. They are example lines, not benchmarks: 80% of customers kept at month 12 and a 12 month payback are Shashwat Ghosh's example lines for a $36,000 contract. Give your own keep line and payback months. If you give none, the answer uses the example lines and says so at the top. The Gemini skill asks for your lines before it runs the check.

What happens to the customer list I upload or paste?

Send customer IDs only, never customer or company names. Your inputs are sent to the CRAFT GTM server, used once to compute the answer, and dropped as soon as the answer returns. Neither your customer rows nor the result are stored on the server. Because the check runs deterministic server rules rather than an AI model, giving it the same inputs on a later day gives the exact same result.
